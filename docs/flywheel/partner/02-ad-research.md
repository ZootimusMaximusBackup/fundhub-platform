## 1. The one-line answer

**This market does not sell training. It sells a business you own that sells money to other businesses — priced anywhere from $95 to $34,495, with the top of the market claiming $25,000 to $100,000.**

The product word they use is "business-in-a-box." Commercial Loan Broker Institute puts it in capitals at the top of their pricing page: "PREMIER BUSINESS-IN-A-BOX PACKAGES WITH REAL RESULTS. HONEST PRICING. NO HIDDEN FEES." (https://commercialloanbrokerinstitute.com/training-program/)

The thing being bought is ownership, not a wage. The same page asks: "READY TO CREATE YOUR FINANCIAL FREEDOM BY OWNING A SUCCESSFUL FINANCE COMPANY?" Their own newer page says it flatter: "Not a course. A brokerage." (https://commercialloanbrokerinstitute.com/kickstart-program/)

Three things are always in the box, at every price point: a list of lenders, some coaching, and a website or customer-tracking tool. A customer-tracking tool is software that keeps a list of who you spoke to and when.

The buyer is someone with no background in lending. Business Lending Blueprint's main headline is "Discover the Proven System Helping Everyday People Launch a Business Loan Brokerage in As Little As 30 Days — Even With Zero Experience..." (https://businesslendingblueprint.com/)

## 3. The angles in play

An "angle" is the one promise an advertisement leads with. Each has a short name so we can talk about it without retyping it.

**Heavily used — three or more companies**

Four companies run the identical two-step: CLBI ("VIEW WEBINAR" next to "SCHEDULE CALL", https://commercialloanbrokerinstitute.com/training-program/), ION Capital ("Click Here To Watch The FREE Masterclass!", https://ioncapitalsolutions.com/free-webinar/), Business Lending Blueprint ("Watch Free Training", https://businesslendingblueprint.com/), and Global Financial Training Program.

**Used by one or two, and doing real work**

- **`business-in-a-box-tiered`** — three named levels that stack, each including the one below. Kick-Start, Executive, Elite (https://commercialloanbrokerinstitute.com/training-program/). This is the strongest-built offer on the board.

## 4. Angles that look worn out

**Read this label first. Every judgement in this section is inference — my reasoning from how many companies say the same thing. It is not a measurement. No competitor's ad spend, click rate or sales were seen, because the ad library was never opened. "Worn out" here means "a buyer shopping around will read this sentence three times", not "this stops working."**

- **`lender-network-access`** — INFERENCE. Every single paid programme I opened leads with access to lenders. That is 8 out of 8: CLBI, Business Lending Blueprint, Funder Intel, Global Financial Training, ION Capital, ICS Loans, Loan Broker Network, The ISO Recruiting Company. Meanwhile four lenders hand the same access over **free**: GoKapital ("$0 Upfront Costs", https://www.gokapital.com/broker-program/), Greenbox Capital, Pearl Capital and United Capital Source. The thing everyone charges for is one click away at no cost.
- **`million-a-year-income`** — INFERENCE, and the strongest case here. MCA University: "LEARN HOW TO MAKE $1 MILLION DOLLARS A YEAR." Direct Business Lending: "$350,000 up to $450,000+ per/year." ION: "$20K+ Per Month." Two separate signs of wear. First, buyers now answer it in public with their losses — on Trustpilot for a company in this exact category: "I lost over $6500 with nothing to show" and "I dropped over $30k when you include advertising expenses" (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/loanbrokernetwork.com). Second, two companies have started selling **against** it: United Capital Source says "No honest program guarantees a monthly figure," and MCA Leads Pro says its rules "specifically prohibit guaranteed funded-deal claims." When rivals start winning by refusing an angle, that angle has stopped separating anyone.
- **`per-deal-commission-math`** — INFERENCE. Five companies lead with a per-deal number: Business Lending Blueprint "$1K–$15K per funded deal", Global Financial Training "up to 15%", Greenbox "up to 19%" (https://www.greenboxcapital.com/company/iso-program/), GoKapital "Up To 6%", Direct Business Lending "2%-10%". The free programmes post the highest number. A paid programme cannot win this comparison.
- **`zero-experience-30-days`** — INFERENCE. The market leader and its nearest rival use the same two words in the same position. A third company arriving with them is invisible.
- **`free-webinar-to-call`** — INFERENCE. Four companies, same two buttons. Anyone entering this way is fourth through the same door.
- **`cheaper-than-the-25k-guys`** — INFERENCE, and weaker than the others. Call it contested rather than dead. Two companies already occupy it. It also blows up in Business Lending Blueprint's face: the sentence "you can actually start your own business for less than $1000 with BLB" sits on the same page as their own "$5500 (Single Payment)". A buyer sees that contradiction in one scroll (https://businesslendingblueprint.com/enroll/).

## 5. What nobody is saying — the gaps

**This is the section to build the offer from.** Each gap was searched for on purpose and came back empty or near-empty. An empty result is a finding, not a failure.

**1. The pain that fills the forums has almost no advertising behind it.**
"Backdooring" is when a lender takes a broker's deal and funds it directly, cutting the broker out. It is all over the DailyFunder forum. On YouTube it barely exists: a search for "MCA backdoored broker funder stole my deal" returned **zero** results. Two videos exist on the whole subject — one with 42 views, one with 263 views. Nobody is selling to the person this happened to.

**2. Nobody answers the obvious objection.**
"Is this market already full?" Search for it and you get five loosely related videos, none of which answer it. Oz Konar — the biggest name in the category — addresses "Is this industry saturated?" only in the small text under a video, never in a title or a picture. No seller has made that objection the hook.

**3. Nobody shows the actual job.**
"A Day in the Life of a Commercial Loan Broker" has 14,940 views and is **four years old**. It is the only on-target result. The generic version of that video — a day in a sales job — pulls 82,000 to 2.5 million views. The demand is proven and the specific version does not exist.

**4. Nobody sells the done-for-you version.**
Searching for a funding agency where the work is done for the buyer returns a 27-view video and a 10-view video. That is the entire field.

**5. Nobody publishes a price where buyers are looking.**
Not one competitor states a price on YouTube. Half of them do not state one on their own website either. Every high-ticket seller in this market makes you get on a call to hear the number. A published price is genuinely open ground.

**6. Nobody offers a real guarantee.**
Business Lending Blueprint's refund needs you to prove you did everything (https://businesslendingblueprint.com/enroll/). CLBI's page — **the page a buyer actually buys from** — has no refund promise at all; the word does not appear on it (https://commercialloanbrokerinstitute.com/training-program/). Their only written refund promise sits on a hidden page and states three different time limits on that one page: 7 days, one week, and 72 hours. An unconditional refund, plainly worded, would be alone in this market.

**7. Nobody qualifies the buyer.**
On CLBI's site, a total stranger can put **$34,495** on a card with no application and no phone call — two clicks from landing page to order form. Turning people away is unoccupied ground.

**8. No credential in this market can be checked.**
CLBI sells a certification promising an "Online Profile" and a "3rd party verification system." There is no public list of holders anywhere, and no address for one on the page. You cannot confirm that one single person holds it (https://commercialloanbrokerinstitute.com/loan-broker-certification/).

**9. Nobody is complaining about lead sellers, in public, on the buyer's side.**
Searching for broker complaints about lead vendors returned only lead vendors advertising, all four to eleven years old. The grievance exists in forums and has no video behind it.

**10. Nobody has been in trouble — which means "the honest one" is open, but unproven.**
I searched on purpose for enforcement action against any named competitor. There is none on the record. Government activity in this category is real but generic — a proposed rule in January 2025 to cover business coaching, and $2 million returned to buyers of money-making programmes in June 2025 — and none of it names anyone on this board. **Do not imply any named competitor is under investigation. None are.**
