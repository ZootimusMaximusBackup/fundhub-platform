## 1. The three hooks I would run first

**1. SHOW-ME-THE-LINE-SHORT**
> "Every shop you are comparing swears it will never backdoor your deals, and so did the one that went quiet on your file and funded your merchant direct."

Run it first because it is the only hook that names the exact thing the buyer cannot check anywhere else, then hands him a way to check it. It also promises only the one demo we can really do on a call: reading the line of code. Nothing in it was flagged.

**2. FUNDER-WENT-QUIET-SHORT**
> "A funder went quiet on your file, then funded your merchant directly. No call, no explanation, nothing you could do."

This is the wound, said plainly, with no claim attached to it. It is the widest net of the three — it works on a broker who has never heard of us. It stays clean because it never says what he will earn.

**3. PRICE-ON-THE-PAGE-SHORT**
> "Four Contact Us forms in this lane and not one of them published a number."

Nobody else in this market can run this hook, because nobody else has a price to print. It sorts hard: a man who wants a number self-selects in one line. It is also the cheapest to prove, because the number is in the contract and in the code.

Why these three and not others: each one attacks a different reason a broker stalls (can't verify the promise / got burned / can't compare prices), so they do not eat each other's audience. All three are short, all three carry zero flagged claims, and all three end on the same one call to action, so the booking page does not change between them.

**One fix before any of them run.** Anywhere the copy says "nothing monthly," change it to the scoped version: *the partner program itself carries no repeating charge*. There is a live monthly menu sold to partners ($297 and $2,497 a month, plus lead flow). The contract is scoped correctly. The copy is not. This is a phrase swap, not a rewrite.
