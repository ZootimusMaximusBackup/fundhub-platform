# COMPLIANCE REVIEW REQUIRED

**This is a closer brief.** It tells people what to say on a sales call. It talks about fees, when money is due, credit file reads, and repair. It must not go to a buyer until a human has read it.

**Hard rules for every closer**

- Never tell a buyer their score will go up.
- Never tell a buyer we will get them funded.
- Never promise a dollar amount from a bank.
- Never promise a negative item will come off.
- Never promise “0% interest,” “no damage to credit,” or “we protect your score.”
- Never make up a win, a client count, or a story. Use only wins Chris has given you in writing.

## 3. Call flow

**[Alec]** His deep-dive had 10 modules (prime, trust, present, pitch, objections, listen for fear, funding facts, follow up, practice, “be the guide”). We keep the **order**. We drop his product talk.

**[Fundhub]** Your job on the call: learn if we can help, pick **one** offer, explain it in plain words, ask for the yes, then send the **contract** and the **pay link**. Use **Present** (the screen you share) when you show the plan.

### Minute 0 — before you join

**[Alec]** Stand up. Get your voice warm. Look at who you are about to talk to.

**[Fundhub]** Open their card. Read:

1. Score they typed
2. Cash they said they have
3. What they want the money for
4. Whether they said they have negatives
5. Time of the call

Write one sentence: “I think this is a funding / course / downsell call because ___.” If you are not sure, you are on a **listen first** call. The $32 read is allowed on any lane.

Do **not** pull their credit before they pay and consent. **[Fundhub]** A typed score is not a bureau score.

### Open (about 2 minutes)

**[Alec]** Thank them. Set the plan. Do not dive into a pitch.

**[Fundhub] Say:**

> “Thanks for being here, [Name]. Here’s how I’ll use your time. I’ll ask a few questions about what you want and where the file is. Then I’ll tell you which path, if any, fits. If nothing fits, I’ll say that. Sound fair?”

Do **not** say we have helped “hundreds” or “3,000+” clients. Alec wrote that. We have no number for you to use unless Chris gave you one. **[Alec — do not use]**

### Listen (most of the call)

**[Alec / Sales 101]** Sales is questions. Find out:

1. What do they want?
2. Why do they want it?
3. How will they feel if they get it?

Talk like a person, not a script robot. Go slow. Listen more than you talk.

**[Fundhub] Ask these, in this order. Soft words. Not a quiz.**

1. “What made you book this — money for something, the file, or both?”
2. “If this works the way you hope, what changes in the next few months?”
3. “What score would you guess you’re near — and is that a guess or did you look?”
4. “Anything on the file that worries you — lates, collections, a lot of looks?”
5. “If we asked you to start with cash today, what is easy vs what would hurt?”  
   (Do **not** say “Do you have $3,000 right now?” as the first money question. **[Alec]** He said scary money questions too early kill the talk. We agree on the *soft ask*. We still must learn if they can pay cash.)
6. “Do you have a business, or is this personal?”  
   (If personal: “That’s fine. You do not need a business to talk to us.” **[Fundhub]**)
7. “Is this a now thing, or a later thing?”

If they are a **maybe** on funding, still finish the listen. Then pick a smaller yes ($32 or a course), not a fake “you’re approved.”

### Name the lane (1 minute)

**[Fundhub]** Say the lane out loud. One sentence.

**Funding**

> “From what you said, the fit to look at is us doing the funding work. That starts at $3,000 cash. The $3,000 is part of a 10% success fee, not on top of it. I cannot promise a lender will say yes.”

**Course**

> “The $3,000 funding start does not fit today — score, cash, or both. What we *can* do is a course you can finance if your score is about 500 or higher. That is not us getting you funded. That is you learning the map.”

**Downsell**

> “I’m not going to sell you the $3,000 today. The honest first step is a $32 file read, or a $200 repair trial if you want us to run one round.”

If **nothing** fits: say so. End kind. Do not invent a deal.

### Explain the one offer (about 2 minutes)

**[Alec]** Explain in under a minute. Outcomes, not jargon. Then a check question: “Does that match what you wanted?”

**[Fundhub]** Use the words in **section 4**. Then ask:

> “Does that match what you wanted, or did I miss something?”

### Future picture — careful

**[Alec]** He had them picture $100,000 and a bigger company in six months. He used pain (“what if nothing changes”).

**[Fundhub]** You may ask what **they** would do with money **if** a lender said yes. You may **not** paint that as a thing we will make happen.

Allowed:

> “If a lender did say yes to the kind of number you have in mind — and that is an if — what would you use it for?”

Not allowed:

> “We’ll get you $100K in a few weeks.”

### Ask for the yes

**[Alec]** Do not leave the ask hanging.

**[Fundhub]**

> “If you want that path, next step is the agreement and the pay link. I can send both while we’re on. Want me to send them?”

Then:

1. Open **Present** if you need the screen.
2. Send the **contract** that matches the offer.
3. Send the **pay link**.
4. Stay on until they pay, they need a minute with a clear next time, or they say no.

One click. Do not smash Present / pay / pull. Soft pull is **$32** and needs their yes and the right consent. **[Fundhub]**

### If they say no or “I need to think”

See **section 5**. Do not bully. Offer a smaller true step or a real stop.
