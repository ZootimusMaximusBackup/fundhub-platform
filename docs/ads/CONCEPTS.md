# The Concept Sheet — 48 concepts

Read these, circle 20, kill the rest. That's the whole ask.

Each one is a hook written the way you'd say it to camera, plus what it's arguing and who it's for.
They're **not scripts yet** — I write the full scripts for the ones you pick, so don't spend time on
anything you already know you won't shoot.

**Work by number.** "Kill 7, 12 and 19, more like 3, rewrite 22 harder" is the fastest way to turn
this. I'll batch your calls and do the whole sheet at once.

## What's here

| | |
|---|---|
| **48 concepts** | Grouped by FICO gate, ranked inside each group |
| **Runtime** | Every one is 60 seconds minimum. 30 at 60–90s, 11 at 90–120s, 7 at 2min+ |
| **Doors** | 31 funded · 8 fixed · 6 sorted · 3 educated |
| **The number to beat** | $32–36 per booked call |

## What I'd bet on if you made me pick five

**1. Who Takes Them Off** · **7. Round Two** · **11. Under 600, Not For You** ·
**26. I Turn Most People Down** · **44. Thirty-Two Dollars**

They cover four different gates and four different mechanisms, so if all five ran you'd learn
something no matter which won.

## Two things to settle before the shoot

1. **Can repair and the $200 trial go on payments?** Your code says yes, your closer pack says cash
   only. Every repair concept is written around the question. Answer it and they get stronger.
2. **Where does the click land** — the VSL page or straight to the two-minute application? Changes
   the CTA on all of them.

## What this set is still light on

- **Proof.** Only two concepts lead with evidence. Koi Poke is one case study carrying everything,
  and it's a funding story — there's no named repair result anywhere.
- **The repair door has no wide-net ad.** All three repair concepts target somebody who already
  knows he has a problem. Nothing catches the person who doesn't.

### 1. Who Takes Them Off

> The guy who got you funded left a pile of hard inquiries on your credit. Ask him when he's taking them off. He's not, because he has no way to.

**Angle** — Every funding company creates the inquiries and not one of them removes them, because removal is a system they never built.  
**Who** — Credit-Protective Planner · 600+  
**Door** — funded · **60-90s** · problem-aware  
**Enemy** — No automated inquiry removal  
**Mechanism** — Automated inquiry removal running inside the belt, not sold as a separate service afterward  
**My bet** — It hands them a question they can go ask their old broker today, and the silence they get back is the ad.

### 2. The Wrong Item First

> Your credit repair guy is fighting the ugliest thing on your report, because that is the only thing he can see. He has never seen the thirty to fifty lenders your file would actually be matched to. So he has no idea whether the item he is fighting is the one standing in the way.

**Angle** — Which negative you fight first is decided by the lender list you were matched to, not by what looks worst on the page. A repair company that has never seen your matched lenders is guessing, and you paid for the guess.  
**Who** — Bank-Rejected Builder, Sitting around 600 with two or three negatives, already paid a… · 600+  
**Door** — fixed · **60-90s** · Solution-aware  
**Enemy** — Enemy 5 - no conveyor belt, no sequencing at all. Repair shops dispute in whatever order the report prints, because nobody ever told them what the file was being prepared for.  
**Mechanism** — The lender matrix sets the dispute queue. Soft pull, tri-bureau, 30-50 matched lenders come back, and the order of disputes is set by which items those specific lenders price. Repair as step A of the belt, not a separate product.  
**My bet** — The guy who already bought repair once and still did not get funded finally hears why it did not work, and that is what gets him on the call.

### 3. Nobody Can Promise A Deletion

> Anybody who promises you a specific item comes off your report is lying to you. Nobody can guarantee that, and the people who say they can are the reason you do not trust a single person in this business anymore. They sold you a promise about your report because they had nothing to say about the lenders on the other side of it.

**Angle** — The honest refusal is the sale. I will not promise you a deletion. I will tell you, before you spend anything, which items are even worth fighting for the lenders you would be matched to, and then I will do the work.  
**Who** — Spam-Scarred Skeptic · 600+  
**Door** — fixed · **60-90s** · Problem-aware  
**Enemy** — Enemy 15 and 16 - everyone is a scammer, I have paid for this before and got nothing. Plus every guaranteed-deletion promise on the internet.  
**Mechanism** — A promise about execution instead of outcome, paired with the $32 soft-pull read that names which items sit between this file and its matched lenders before a dollar changes hands.  
**My bet** — Refusing the promise everybody else makes is the fastest trust build available, and this buyer's whole problem is that he cannot tell who is lying.

### 4. The No Is Still Talking

> The bank that told you no last year is still talking. That inquiry sits on your file, and the next lender reads it before it reads anything about your business. We take it off before you go back in.

**Mechanism** — The Conveyor Belt, Step A applied to historic inquiries — the old trail comes off before the first new application goes out  
**My bet** — The Bank-Rejected Builder thinks the denial is over and forgotten, and finding out it is still costing them is a genuine gut punch.

### 5. The Handoff Nobody Bills You For

> The thing that costs you is not the dispute. It is the dead time after an item comes off, while the repair company gets around to telling the funding company. Two businesses that have never spoken to each other, and you are paying both of them.

**Angle** — Repair and funding bought as two separate vendors means every win sits there going stale. The item clears and nothing happens, because the handoff is a human remembering to send an email.  
**Who** — Credit-Protective Planner, who has already tried running repair and funding as two separate… · 600+  
**Door** — fixed · **60-90s** · Solution-aware  
**Enemy** — Enemy 4 and 10 - no automated credit repair, and no support system behind the sale. It gets done by hand, slowly, or not at all.  
**Mechanism** — The event-driven engine. Do not put a number on the dead time - there is no measured figure for it. State it as dead time and let the mechanism answer it.  
**My bet** — Naming a cost nobody has ever charged for, the gap between two vendors, is a brand new pain, and only an operation that owns both halves can run this hook without indicting itself.
