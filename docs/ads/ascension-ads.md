# Ascension funnel — the ads

The pages are the middle of the funnel. This is the front of it.

Written against the Jeremy Haynes direct-response system: `Hook → Reasons → CTA`,
in-market first, dynamic creative, half short and half long.

**COMPLIANCE REVIEW REQUIRED.** Nothing here runs until a person approves it. Read the
guardrails at the bottom before writing a single new variation — this is a regulated
consumer-finance product being advertised on platforms with their own financial-services
rules on top.

---

## 1. Which strategy, and the honest reason

The framework's table sends a high-ticket offer to **Venus Fly Trap 2.0** — three content
steps, five long videos each, then the direct-response offer. That is the right answer for
a $10,000 offer *when you already have a content library.*

**We have no videos at all.** So VFT 2.0 cannot start on day one, and pretending otherwise
would burn budget waiting on content nobody has shot.

**Start here instead: Paid Leads (low-ticket to high-ticket).** It is the one strategy in the
system that needs no content library, and the ladder is already built for it:

> Sell something cheap and genuinely useful at break-even. What comes back is a **buyer**,
> not a freeloader. Then the buyers go to closers.

So: **run direct response straight at the $27 Decline Autopsy.** Every buyer is someone who
had declines, paid to understand them, and has now seen how we read a file. That is a far
warmer list for a $10,000 partnership than any cold audience, and it was paid for at break-even
rather than bought as a lead.

**Then layer The Forester** as content gets shot — content cycle bin on cold, anyone who
watches ten seconds becomes warm, direct response runs at the warm audience. That is the
bridge to VFT 2.0 later, and it is worth building toward because warm traffic is the cheaper
audience by a wide margin.

**Do not run cold traffic straight at the $10,000 page.** A $10k offer sold to a stranger with
no proof, no content and no video is the most expensive way to learn this lesson.

## 2. Who we are actually talking to

The system's rule is that in-market beats needs-convinced, and in-market people **do not need
telling that funding is a real business.** They need to know *which* and *who*.

Four in-market segments, each with a different circumstance:

| # | Segment | What they already believe | The circumstance to name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brokers sending deals to a funder** | Funding makes money — they watch it happen | They found the client and rent the relationship |
| 2 | **Affiliates on a referral cut** | The offer converts — they've been paid on it | It pays, but it isn't a business they own |
| 3 | **Operators who tried to build it** | The model works — they attempted it | They stalled on lenders, underwriting or staff |
| 4 | **Closers with no book of their own** | They can sell this — they do it daily | They're selling somebody else's company |

Segment 3 is the highest-intent and the smallest. Segment 1 is the biggest. Start with 1 and 3.

**Do not open with "is funding a good business?"** That is needs-convinced messaging. It
explodes cost per lead and fills calls with people who cannot bring a deal.

## 3. Ad set A — cold → $27 Decline Autopsy

This is the workhorse. It buys the buyer.

**Hook variations** (shoot all six; dynamic creative on; let the platform pick):

1. *You already know* — "You already know why most funding deals get declined. You just never get told which reason killed yours."
2. *You're doing this, but* — "You're submitting files and getting one-word declines back. That word is a category, not a cause."
3. *Circumstance* — "You've got a folder of dead deals on your desktop right now. Some of them aren't dead."
4. *Qualify inside the hook* — "If you've submitted more than ten funding files this year, this is for you. If you haven't, it isn't."
5. *Specificity* — "'Does not meet criteria' is not a reason. Somebody just didn't finish the sentence."
6. *Real-time contextual* — "Still guessing why that last file came back declined?"

**Reasons — emotional then logical, in that order:**

- *Emotional:* you did the work, found the client, built the file, and got a category back. You can't learn from a category. So you guess, and you carry the same mistake into the next file.
- *Logical:* send the declines. You get the actual cause of death per file, which ones were dead on arrival, which are still alive today, and the pattern across all of them — the mistake you're repeating.
- *Why us:* reading files is the whole business. It's hard to describe and easy to show, which is why it's $27 instead of a sales call.

**CTA + so-you-can:** "Send your declines below — so you can stop guessing which mistake you're repeating. Twenty-seven dollars, nothing recurring."

**Lengths:** three at 30 seconds, three at 60–90. Same hooks, different depth.

## 4. Ad set B — buyers and warm → $10,000 partnership

Runs at autopsy buyers, board subscribers, trial takers, and anyone who watched deep on set A.
**Never at cold.**

**Hook variations:**

1. *You already know* — "You already know funding works. You've just been building somebody else's company with it."
2. *You're doing this, but* — "You send the deal, they keep the client. The renewal is theirs. The back end is theirs."
3. *Circumstance* — "You can close a funding deal. You just don't own a funding company."
4. *Disqualify openly* — "This one has a floor: ten funding clients a month. If that's not you, skip it."
5. *The objection first* — "No, you don't have to get funded yourself to run a funding company."

**Reasons:**

- *Emotional:* deal flow is one job. Fulfillment is a completely different company. Stalling on the second one isn't a hustle problem.
- *Logical:* your brand on the front, our engine behind it. 50% of funding and 50% of repair, front end and back end, including half the 10% success fee. It never moves. $10,000 once, no monthly base fee.
- *Trust:* a person decides on a review call — never a lender and never a score. Financing exists down to 405 FICO, and it's how you pay, not how you qualify.

**CTA + so-you-can:** "Apply for a review call below — so you can find out whether your deal flow carries this before you spend anything."

**Lengths:** longer skews better here. Two at 60 seconds, four at 90–150.

## 5. Ad set C — retargeting, 72 hours before a review call

The system calls this **Hammer Them**: 15–20 pieces in 72 hours ahead of the call, so the call
opens warm instead of cold. Five categories, and each one here answers a real objection:

| Category | The piece |
|---|---|
| **Authority** | How a file actually gets read — the thing the $27 autopsy demonstrates |
| **Objection** | "Which lenders do you use?" — the real answer, and why it stays that way |
| **Objection** | The ten-a-month floor, said plainly, including who shouldn't apply |
| **Objection** | "Do I need to get funded myself?" — no, and why people keep assuming yes |
| **Mechanism** | What fulfillment actually removes from your plate, step by step |
| **Client results** | *Blocked until real, verifiable results exist. Do not fabricate one.* |

## 6. What to watch, and what to do about it

The framework's rule: creative is the number one lever. Not targeting, not bidding, not budget.
So when something is wrong, change the creative first.

| Symptom | Most likely cause | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| CTR under 2% | The hook | Swap hooks before anything else |
| Cheap clicks, no $27 buyers | Page/ad mismatch | Make the page headline echo the ad hook (framework Part 4) |
| $27 buyers, no applications | Ladder gap | The board and trial are the bridge — they're built |
| Applications, weak calls | Gatekeeping too soft | Push the disqualifiers earlier and harder |
| Worked, then died | Ad fatigue | Rotate creative — the system says every 72 hours once performance drops |

Volume expectation, Andromeda era: **15–20 diverse creatives a week minimum.** Diverse in
*message*, not fifteen edits of one video.

## 7. Guardrails — read before writing any variation

Non-negotiable, and they come from the offer terms and from this being consumer finance:

- **No income claims.** The 50% split is a commercial term, not an earnings projection. Never
  "make $X", never a partner income figure, never implied.
- **No credit-outcome claims.** Never promise a score change, an approval, or a funding amount
  for anyone.
- **No guaranteed funding**, for a partner or for their clients.
- **Never name or hint at a lender.** Not in an ad, not in a comment reply, not on a call.
- **Never fabricate a result or testimonial.** People do contact the person in a testimonial.
  Where proof doesn't exist yet, run without proof.
- **Marketing is a paid add-on** — never imply it's included in the $10,000.
- **A partner sees a partner view, not the whole CRM.** Don't let an ad promise more.
- **Never gate on the partner being funded themselves**, and don't imply it.
- Financial-services advertising rules apply on top of all of the above. A person approves
  every variation before it runs.
