# Ascension funnel — the ads

The pages are the middle of the funnel. This is the front of it.

Written against the Jeremy Haynes ad system, read in full — the strategy SOPs, the
direct-response creation framework, and the If This Then That troubleshooting rules.
Where a number appears below it is from those SOPs, not invented.

**COMPLIANCE REVIEW REQUIRED.** Nothing here runs until a person approves it. The
guardrails are section 8 — read them before writing any variation. This is a regulated
consumer-finance product being advertised on platforms with their own financial-services
rules stacked on top.

---

## 1. Which strategy — and why the obvious answer is wrong for us right now

The system's own table sends a high-ticket offer to **Venus Fly Trap 2.0**. That is the
correct long-term answer for a $10,000 offer. It is not the day-one answer, for two
reasons the SOP states outright:

- It needs **15 long-form videos** — five per step, three steps.
- The SOP says plainly: **"This isn't a $50/day strategy."** Its worked example is
  **$60,000 across 30 days**, with half or more of it parked in Step 1.

**We have zero videos.** So VFT 2.0 cannot start, and neither can The Forester, which
needs **5–12 videos** of its own.

### Start here: Paid Leads, cold → the $27 autopsy

The one strategy in the system that needs **no content library**, and the ladder is
already built for it:

> Sell something cheap and genuinely useful at break-even. What comes back is a
> **buyer**, not a freeloader. Then the buyers go to closers.

So run direct response straight at the **$27 Decline Autopsy**. Every buyer is somebody
who had declines, paid to understand them, and has now watched how we read a file. That
is a far warmer list for a $10,000 partnership than any cold audience, and it was bought
at break-even instead of paid for as a lead.

**Do not run cold traffic at the $10,000 page.** No proof, no video, no content, highest
price on the ladder.

### Then, as content gets shot: The Forester

The Forester's whole purpose is CPM arbitrage — turn cold into warm for about **$0.01 per
engagement** (the SOP records as low as $0.0001), then run direct response at the warm
audience, because warm CPMs are lower. Real setup from the SOP:

- **Content Cycle Bin 1:** one campaign, **5–12 ad sets, one video per ad set**,
  **ad set budget optimization**, objective **Engagement**. Same cold targeting in every
  ad set — a lookalike stack of past customers, past qualified leads and the qualified
  email list, or an ideal-buyer cold interest set if those don't exist yet.
- **From $5/day per ad set** upward. The SOP's screenshot example runs $30/day per ad
  set across 9 ad sets.
- **10 seconds of any one video = warm.** Build the audience on **365-day** windows —
  a wider audience costs less. Include every engagement option: visited profile, engaged
  with post or ad, pressed a button, messaged the page, saved a post.
- **Then run direct response at that warm audience AND Content Cycle Bin 2 at the same
  time.** Bin 2 is built identically to Bin 1. Match the per-ad-set spend on warm to
  cold — the SOP flags a client who ran $30/day cold and only $5/day warm as the mistake.
- The SOP's framing matters: **it is a spider web, not a sequence.** Let the platform
  decide which video each person sees. Do not force an order.

**Only then, VFT 2.0**, once 15 long-form videos and a real budget exist.

## 2. Who we are actually talking to

The system's rule is that the **3–4% who are in-market** convert cheapest, and that
in-market people **already believe the category works.** They need *which* and *who*, not
*whether*. Opening with "is funding a good business?" is needs-convinced messaging —
the SOP says it explodes cost per lead, lowers show rates and wrecks sales calls.

| # | Segment | Already believes | Circumstance to name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brokers sending deals to a funder | Funding makes money — they watch it happen | They found the client and rent the relationship |
| 2 | Affiliates on a referral cut | The offer converts — they've been paid on it | It pays, but it isn't a business they own |
| 3 | Operators who tried to build it | The model works — they attempted it | They stalled on lenders, underwriting or staff |
| 4 | Closers with no book of their own | They can sell this — they do it daily | They're selling somebody else's company |

Segment 3 is highest-intent and smallest. Segment 1 is biggest. Start with 1 and 3.

## 3. Ad set A — cold → $27 Decline Autopsy

The workhorse. It buys the buyer. **Dynamic creative on**; supply all six hooks and let
the platform mix. **Half short, half long** — three at 30 seconds, three at 60–90.

**Hooks**, each built on a named opener from the framework:

1. *You Already Know* — "You already know why most funding deals get declined. You just never get told which reason killed yours."
2. *You're Doing This, But* — "You're submitting files and getting one-word declines back. That word is a category, not a cause."
3. *Circumstance* — "You've got a folder of dead deals on your desktop right now. Some of them aren't dead."
4. *Qualify inside the ad* — "If you've submitted more than ten funding files this year, this is for you. If you haven't, it isn't."
5. *Specificity* — "'Does not meet criteria' is not a reason. Somebody just didn't finish the sentence."
6. *Real-time contextual* — "Still guessing why that last file came back declined?"

**Body — emotional first, then logical**, per the framework:

- *Emotional:* you found the client, built the file, and got a category back. You can't learn from a category, so you guess, and you carry the same mistake into the next file.
- *Logical:* send the declines. You get the cause of death per file, which were dead on arrival, which are still alive today, and the pattern across all of them.
- *Why us:* reading files is the whole business. Hard to describe, easy to show — which is why it's $27 and not a sales call.

**CTA + so-you-can:** "Send your declines below — so you can stop guessing which mistake you're repeating. Twenty-seven dollars, nothing recurring."

## 4. Ad set B — buyers and warm → $10,000 partnership

Runs at autopsy buyers, board subscribers, trial takers, and deep viewers from set A.
**Never at cold.** Longer skews better here: two at 60 seconds, four at 90–150.

**Hooks:**

1. *You Already Know* — "You already know funding works. You've just been building somebody else's company with it."
2. *You're Doing This, But* — "You send the deal, they keep the client. The renewal is theirs. The back end is theirs."
3. *Circumstance* — "You can close a funding deal. You just don't own a funding company."
4. *Disqualify openly* — "This one has a floor: ten funding clients a month. If that's not you, skip it."
5. *Objection first* — "No, you don't have to get funded yourself to run a funding company."

**Body:**

- *Emotional:* deal flow is one job. Fulfillment is a different company. Stalling on the second isn't a hustle problem.
- *Logical:* your brand on the front, our engine behind it. 50% of funding and 50% of repair, front end and back end, including half the 10% success fee. It never moves. $10,000 once, no monthly base fee.
- *Trust:* a person decides on a review call — never a lender, never a score. Financing exists down to 405 FICO and is how you pay, not how you qualify.

**CTA + so-you-can:** "Apply for a review call below — so you can find out whether your deal flow carries this before you spend anything."

## 5. Ad set C — Hammer Them, the 72 hours before a review call

**15–20+ pieces inside 72 hours** before the call. The mechanism the SOP names is
familiarity bias and the mere-exposure effect — and the key strategic point:
**you are selling the next step (showing up), not the offer.**

Format, per the SOP: **short, direct, vertical videos built for Reels**, posted organically
to Instagram and cross-posted to Facebook. **Do not use copyrighted music** — it blocks
you from running them through paid engagement campaigns later.

The SOP's **four pillars**, mapped to this offer:

| Pillar | Pieces to make |
|---|---|
| **Questions** | Why we're opening the engine to partners at all · how the 50/50 actually works · what fulfillment covers, step by step |
| **Second-layer questions** | What "a partner view, not the whole CRM" means in practice · what the ad-account connection is for · how add-ons get priced |
| **Objections** | "Which lenders?" — the real answer and why it stays that way · the ten-a-month floor, including who shouldn't apply · "do I need to get funded myself?" — no · "is marketing included?" — no |
| **Expectations** | What the review call is and who's on it · what day one looks like · what the first thirty days look like |

**Client results:** blocked until real, verifiable results exist. Do not fabricate one.

The SOP's own warning applies: this strategy is not for people who won't make content.

## 6. If this, then that — the real rules

Straight from the troubleshooting SOP, with its numbers.

| Symptom | The rule |
|---|---|
| **Low conversions anywhere** | Find the bottleneck before touching ads. Compute the conversion rate at every step, find the choke point, fix that one. Repeat. |
| **CTR under 2%** | 2% is the line. Rotate creatives every **72 hours** until it clears — new hooks, headlines, body, dynamic vs static. Any change is a good change while testing. |
| **Sub-2% CTR but profitable** | **Leave it alone. Never turn off something profitable.** Duplicate it and test new audiences with the same creative instead. |
| **Learning limited** | If you came close to the conversion threshold, relaunch the ad set. If you were far off, fix the funnel bottleneck first. Still stuck: test new audiences with the best creative, or run cyclic campaigns optimized for each funnel step. |
| **CPA too high** | Audiences under **1,000,000 people** are the usual cause — widen first. If you can't, cut the daily budget 20% until CPA drops, then scale back **10–20% a day**. |
| **CPA spiked after scaling** | You scaled too hard. Anything above a **10–30%** daily budget increase on automated bidding spikes CPA. To go $200/day → $1,000/day: **duplicate the campaign, set the new one to $1,000, then switch the old one off** so the audiences don't overlap. |
| **No-shows on review calls** | **20% or less is good, 30% is ok, above 35% is unacceptable.** That's what ad set C exists to fix. Add the cost of no-shows into your real cost per booked call — most people don't. |
| **Worked, then died** | Ad fatigue. Rotate creative. |

**Volume, Andromeda era:** 15–20+ diverse creatives a week minimum — diverse in *message*,
not fifteen edits of one video.

**And the first principle behind all of it:** creative is the number one lever. Not
targeting, not bidding, not budget. When something is wrong, change the creative first.

## 7. Funnel alignment

The framework requires the page to echo the ad — headline, hero visual, and subhead all
matching what the person just clicked. Our pages are already written to in-market openers,
so keep ad hooks and page headlines in sync when either changes. A hook that beats the
page headline should become the page headline.

## 8. Guardrails — read before writing any variation

From the offer terms and from this being consumer finance:

- **No income claims.** The 50% split is a commercial term, not an earnings projection. Never "make $X", never a partner income figure, never implied.
- **No credit-outcome claims.** Never promise a score change, an approval, or a funding amount, for a partner or their clients.
- **No guaranteed funding**, ever.
- **Never name or hint at a lender.** Not in an ad, not in a comment reply, not on a call.
- **Never fabricate a result or testimonial.** People do contact the person in a testimonial. Where proof doesn't exist, run without proof.
- **Marketing is a paid add-on** — never imply it's included in the $10,000.
- **A partner sees a partner view, not the whole CRM.** No ad may promise more.
- **Never gate on the partner being funded themselves**, and never imply it.
- Financial-services advertising policy applies on top of all of the above. A person approves every variation before it runs.
