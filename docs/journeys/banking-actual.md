## 1. The three steps of connecting a bank

The important thing a non-coder should take from this picture: **this platform
never sees the client's bank password.** Step 2 happens entirely between the
client and their own bank, inside Plaid's window. Nothing comes back to us except
a one-time ticket.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Staff opens a client's file] --> B[POST action: link_token]
    B --> C{Is Plaid set up<br/>on this deploy?}
    C -->|No keys set| D[503 not_configured<br/>names the missing variable<br/>NEVER dials out]
    C -->|No consent scope set| E[503 not_configured<br/>PLAID_PRODUCTS unset<br/>NEVER dials out]
    C -->|Yes| F[Ask Plaid for a link token]
    F --> G[Browser opens Plaid Link]

    G --> H[CLIENT signs in at THEIR OWN BANK<br/>inside Plaid's window]
    H --> I[Plaid hands the browser<br/>a one-time public token]

    I --> J[POST action: link]
    J --> K{Is the encryption<br/>key set?}
    K -->|No| L[503 — refuses BEFORE the trade<br/>so no bank key is created<br/>that we cannot store]
    K -->|Yes| M[Trade the one-time ticket<br/>for a lasting bank key]
    M -->|Plaid refuses| N[Write an audit row: who tried, what failed<br/>then report the failure]
    M -->|Plaid agrees| O[SCRAMBLE the key, locked to this one client]
    O --> P[Save the connection<br/>ciphertext only, never the plain key]
    P --> Q[Write an audit row: a bank key now exists]
    Q --> R[Immediately read the balances]
```

**Why the two refusals at the top matter.** Both stop before any request leaves
the building. A deploy with no keys cannot accidentally reach Plaid, and a deploy
where nobody has chosen what to ask the client for cannot open the consent screen
at all.

## 2. Reading the balances

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Someone asks for a refresh] --> B{Does this connection<br/>still have a key?}
    B -->|No| C[409 — the client must connect again<br/>retrying cannot help]
    B -->|Yes| D[Unscramble the key<br/>the ONLY place this happens]
    D --> E[Ask Plaid for the balances]

    E -->|Plaid refuses| F[Mark the connection<br/>with what went wrong]
    F --> G{Which kind<br/>of problem?}
    G -->|Bank password changed| H[login_required<br/>ONLY the client can fix this]
    G -->|Consent running out| I[pending_expiration]
    G -->|Client turned us off| J[revoked — never retry]
    G -->|Something else| K[error + Plaid's own words kept]
    H --> L[Write an audit row: who tried, what failed]
    I --> L
    J --> L
    K --> L

    E -->|Plaid answers| M[Save each account<br/>balances as whole cents]
    M --> N{Did Plaid return<br/>ZERO accounts?}
    N -->|Yes| O[Close NOTHING<br/>a blip must not empty someone's finances<br/>record it for a human to see]
    N -->|No| P[Any account that vanished<br/>is marked closed, NOT deleted<br/>its history survives]
    O --> Q[Write an audit row:<br/>which accounts, no figures]
    P --> Q
```

**The rule that is easiest to break and hardest to notice:** saving balances
never touches whether an account is personal or business. A refresh that reset
that would silently undo every classification anyone had ever made.

## 3. Whose money is it?

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[A newly connected account] --> B[Marked UNKNOWN]
    B --> C{Does Plaid say whether<br/>it is personal or business?}
    C -->|Plaid never says| D[It STAYS unknown<br/>the code does not guess<br/>even if the account is named<br/>Business Checking]

    D --> E[A named person decides]
    E --> F{What did they say?}
    F -->|Personal| G[personal<br/>any business link is cleared]
    F -->|Business| H[business<br/>may name which business]
    F -->|Cannot tell| I[unknown — but now ATTRIBUTED<br/>somebody looked and could not tell<br/>is not the same as nobody looked]

    G --> J[Record WHO decided and WHEN]
    H --> J
    I --> J

    J --> K[A later balance refresh<br/>leaves all of this alone]

    L[The database itself refuses:] --> M[a made-up category]
    L --> N[a business named on a personal account]
    L --> O[a decision with no author]
```

**Unknown is a real answer, not a blank to be tidied away.** Anything built on
top of this must handle three answers, not two. Treating unknown as personal is
how a client's private savings ends up filed under their company.

## 5. What never leaves

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[The bank key] --> B[Scrambled before it touches storage]
    B --> C[Never returned by any endpoint]
    B --> D[Never written to any log]
    B --> E[Never placed in an error message]
    B --> F[Never appears in the audit trail]
    G[There is no 'show me the key' function.<br/>An SSN has one, because a human sometimes has to<br/>read one to a credit bureau. Nobody ever has to<br/>read a bank key.] --> A
```

The audit trail records **which** accounts were read — their last four digits,
their type, and whether a balance was present — and never **how much** was in
them. An audit trail is not a second copy of somebody's finances.
