# 1. THE AVATAR METHODOLOGY

## 1.1 The problem it exists to solve

From *#6 The Evolve Avatar Training*, verbatim:

> "For most brands, Avatars, ICPs (Ideal customer profiles), or Personas are just demographic-based & generalized profiles that you create once because someone in marketing told you they were important. You may think about them once or twice when making ads, but very rarely do people actually think through an Avatar when writing ads. And that is the biggest problem. **Most Avatars are NOT actionable.**"

> "But the truth is, you are actually targeting Avatars with every single ad you write. No matter what you write, you are targeting an Avatar. The problem is, the Avatar you're targeting is usually super broad. And it's not relatable. **Which is why most people suck at making ads.**"

The definition and the goal:

> "**Avatar:** A group of specific people most likely to buy your product."
>
> "**Goal:** Create *instant* relatability - when they read your ad, they think 'that's exactly me.'"
>
> "**Why Relatability Matters:** When people feel like you really get them, they trust you. When they trust you, they buy your stuff."

The mental model:

> "My favorite way to explain Avatars is 'memes' or 'relatable videos' online... Why was it relatable? Because that video called out a specific Avatar. And you identified with it. And as a result, you probably laughed at it or said 'That is definitely me.'"

Why it matters more now:

> "**Meta targeting is now done 99% by the actual creative. Not the audience you select at the adset level.** ... So, if you want to target an avatar, it has to be done through the creative. Not by selecting interest on Meta. Meta also rewards relevance. Which means the MORE your creative resonates with your specific avatar, the lower your costs will be and the higher your conversions."

## 1.2 The Avatar Scale

> "Avatars exist on a **scale** that has essentially **infinite combinations.** Meaning… You can create avatars so SPECIFIC, it alienates a single person. Yet on the other side of the scale you can create avatars so BROAD, it includes everyone."

> "If there are essentially infinite combinations of Avatars… That means there will ALWAYS be untapped combinations (aka Avatars) that you can target."

**The Rules Of Thumb** (verbatim):

> * "Avoid combining more than one Desire when building your Avatar."
> * "The more of these categories you use, the more specific your Avatar will be. The fewer categories you use, the less specific your Avatar will be."
> * "The more BROAD your Avatar is: The better your product, offer, brand, and marketing skillset have to be to get conversions."
> * "The more SPECIFIC but not ALIENATING your Avatar is: The worse your product, offer, brand, and marketing skillset can be to get conversions."
> * "*This does not mean sell a shit product. It means if you have nothing 'insanely innovative' about your product, aka it's more sophisticated, you need to have a more specific avatar to build trust.*"

## 1.3 THE CORE 5 CATEGORIES — the framework itself

> "I have developed what I am calling the **Core 5 categories**, in which you can group people to create avatars... you can pull from each of these 5 categories to 'make a combination'. Again, just to be clear, by combination, I mean avatar."

> "To make an avatar that doesn't suck, you must understand that **the order of these categories is what matters most** for building EFFECTIVE Avatars. Effective Avatars are avatars that SCALE and MAKE YOU MONEY."

> "The coolest part about how this training shows you how to build Avatars is that you can have avatars that have **zero mentions of demographics.**"

> "So, with traditional 'Avatar building strategies' People ALWAYS start with demographics. And that's why most people suck at making Avatars & never use them. So, if you want to create Avatars that your competition is not, and more importantly, Avatars that make you a ton of cash… You then need to focus on the top categories first. **And actually use demographics LAST.**"

> "The reason this gives you an advantage in the market and also helps you create specific Avatars that are more scalable is that you are not alienating people based on logistics. And if you don't alienate people just based on their demographics, it opens up a larger opportunity for you to scale and have more success by things that ACTUALLY drive real purchase intent."

The order is fixed: **1. Desires → 2. Experiences → 3. Emotions → 4. Behaviors & Habits → 5. Demographics.**

> "Through a lot of head scratching, back & forth, and proof checking with AI, I have been able to simplify this down into **5 CATEGORIES**. There is NO REASON to complicate it more than that for your Avatars."

### CATEGORY 1 — DESIRES

> "Desires are the 'I want/I need' statements that you've already been hunting for."

> "To be clear, grouping people by the CORE DESIRE is not recommended for building avatars, as those are too broad. And again, the broader your avatar, the better everything else has to be."

> "Now, it is STILL very important that you understand the CORE DESIRE of the more surface-level desires that you find through research. However, for the sake of building avatars initially, I recommend using more surface-level desires because they are more specific and help build better avatars."

> "**VERY IMPORTANT:** You should always start your Avatar creation process with a DESIRE over a demographic. Desire-first Avatars are stronger than demographic-first Avatars due to the emotional pull that desires have. AKA desires drive stronger purchase behavior than demographics alone."

Worked examples of surface-level desires with their core desire named:

> 1. "I want to get better sleep *(Core Desire: Health)*"
> 2. "I want my dog to stop pulling *(Core Desire: Control/Comfort/Status)*"
> 3. "I need to lose this baby fat *(Core Desire: Health/Status)*"
> 4. "I want to be in control of my body *(Core Desire: Control/Health)*"
> 5. "I want to perform better in the bedroom *(Core Desire: Sex/Control)*"

> "As you can see, a lot of these surface-level desires actually may attend to multiple core desires depending on the context. This is why you must HAVE an understanding of CORE DESIRES as well. And if possible, in your research, try to dig even deeper to understand the 'Why' behind the surface-level desire, as that will reveal what the core desire actually is."

**THE CHECKER:**

> "your 'formula' / 'checker' to make sure that you actually HAVE a desire, is to use the '**I want X…**' or '**I need Y…**' statement. **If you can't say that in a sentence, it's not a desire.**"

Worked example:

> "When doing research, someone says, *'Why does it take so long to brush your teeth?'* The desire you could pull from this is: 'I want to brush my teeth faster.' There you go, now you have a desire."

### CATEGORY 2 — EXPERIENCES

> "Experiences are situations that people have gone through in the past or are currently going through. For effective avatar building, experiences are split into 2 categories:"

> "**1. Situational Experiences** - Circumstances or situations the person is in or has gone through"
> — "Bedroom gets very bright in the morning / Dog pulls on walks / Had a baby / Lived in a small apartment with thin walls / Got divorced two years ago"
>
> "these experiences could be ACTIVE (which usually creates more urgency if it is a problem) or they could be long in the PAST (which usually creates more relatability through nostalgia)"

> "**2. Product-Based Experiences:** Experiences where a solution/product was tried and a specific outcome resulted."
> — "Tried blackout curtains and still woke up tired / Used a dog harness and it didn't work / Lost 30 pounds on keto diet but gained it back / Hired a contractor and got burned"

> "Remember that product experiences will always have an outcome. EVEN if the outcome is 'I'm still trying it' or 'I'm still testing it' that is the outcome."

**THE ISOLATION RULE** — this is what makes the categories work:

> "For this category in general, but especially with product experiences, we need to focus specifically on WHAT is happening... For the sake of CORE AVATARS, we need to **ISOLATE the experience.**"
>
> "Try to avoid including:
> A. What they WANT as a result → *That is their desire*
> B. Not HOW they feel about it → *That is their emotions*
> C. Not their RESPONSE to it → *That is their behaviors*"

Worked example of the decomposition:

> "'Tried blackout curtains and still woke up tired' — This has an IMPLIED *Desire* that the person wants to sleep better (but it's not 100% true). This also has an implied *Emotion* that the person is frustrated. This also has an implied *Behavior* that the person is looking for a better way to sleep. But for this section, we ONLY CARE about WHAT is happening or WHAT happened."

**THE CHECKER (2-question test):**

> "Does this describe a circumstance or event? Did we remove the EMOTION from it?"
>
> ✅ Pure Experiences: "Just had a baby" / "Lives in a noisy apartment" / "Tried Ozempic and it worked"
> ❌ Not Pure: "Wants to have another baby after having a new baby" *(Experience + desire)* / "Hates living in a noisy apartment" *(experience + emotion)*
>
> "The demographic overlap is fine - 'just had a baby' naturally implies female demographics, which is realistic."

Why experiences matter beyond the avatar:

> "If you've ever wondered why market sophistication exists, it's because of experiences across the masses… EVEN if the experiences are INDIRECT, meaning 'We know someone who tried a dog harness and it didn't work'. Those are called 'Direct experiences' VS 'Indirect experiences'."
>
> "experiences essentially shape everything about us."

**The Disneyland demonstration** (how one experience becomes two opposite sub-avatars, and how analogy reaches an avatar your product has no literal link to):

> "**Core Avatar:** Someone who went to Disneyland → This is an Avatar JUST based on an experience
> **Sub Avatar #1:** Someone who went to Disneyland and feels that it is overrated → *narrowed down by emotion*
> **Sub Avatar #2:** Someone who went to Disneyland and feels like there is no better place in the world"
>
> "**We could say:** 'It's like going to Disneyland every day' → and that would REALLY resonate with Sub Avatar #2. **We could also say:** 'It's like if Disneyland was actually fun.' → That would likely resonate with Sub Avatar #1."

### CATEGORY 3 — EMOTIONS

> "Emotions are how people feel or states they enter as a result of the experiences, desires, and behaviors they have."
>
> "Remember, the goal of this category is to build relatability with our Avatar. **Not to be a therapist.**"
>
> "in marketing, emotions are one of the most powerful ways to group people... **Emotions a lot of the time can bypass logical thinking.** Most people 'logically' would not slash someone else's tires… But when emotions are high *(for example from a breakup)* then all of a sudden, slashing tires doesn't seem like a terrible idea."

**Primary emotions** — six, universal:

> "Primary emotions are the MAIN emotions that are universal to all... Think of them like facial expressions. **You can literally make all 6 of these as facial expressions.**"

**The governing law:**

> "**Emotions are universal, *triggers* are individual.** Every human knows what fear feels like in their body. But, what activates that fear is completely different from person to person (which is great for Avatar creation)."

**Secondary emotions** — a signal to dig, never a stopping point:

> "Secondary emotions are your brain's attempt to label and organize your feelings in a way that is easier for you to understand. They're like emotional shortcuts... **whenever you see a secondary emotion. It's just a sign for you to dig deeper.** So, if someone doesn't specifically state a primary emotion or a version of it, you need to go deeper and unpack it & find out WHY they feel that way."

The decoder, verbatim:

> "**Anxious** → They feel anxious because they likely 'fear' a specific experience. Or are future pacing 'Sadness' as an outcome that has not happened yet.
> **Frustrated** → They feel frustrated because maybe they felt 'joy' in an experience and then that 'joy' went away... Or they have a specific desire but can't attain it so they experience 'anger' of things failing which leads to frustration.
> **Overwhelmed** → They feel overwhelmed because they likely 'fear' they can't handle all the demands/responsibilities. Or they're experiencing 'anger' at having too much put on them with no control over the situation.
> **Confident** → They feel confident because they likely experienced 'joy' from past successes in similar situations. Or they have specific evidence/experiences that removed their 'fear' about the outcome.
> **Excited** → They feel excited because they're anticipating 'joy' from a future experience or outcome.
> **Stressed** → They feel stressed because they 'fear' the consequences of not meeting expectations/deadlines. Or they're experiencing 'anger' at the pressure/demands being placed on them.
> **Motivated** → They feel motivated because they're anticipating 'joy' from achieving their goal. Or they 'fear' the consequences of NOT taking action, so they're driven to move.
> **Worried** → They feel worried because they 'fear' a specific negative outcome. Very similar to anxious - they're future pacing potential loss or 'sadness.'
> **Disappointed** → They feel disappointed because they experienced 'sadness' from unmet expectations. They anticipated 'joy' from something that didn't deliver.
> **Insecure** → They feel insecure because they 'fear' judgment, rejection, or not being good enough. Or they've experienced 'sadness' from past failures that make them doubt themselves."

> "**IMPORTANT takeaway here is this:** The entire goal of understanding emotions is so you can truly understand how someone is feeling, so you know how to speak to them in your ad."

**THE CHECKER:**

> "Does this describe how a person feels ABOUT or IN RESPONSE to something? From there, if it is NOT one of the primary 6 emotions, ask WHY and WHAT to really figure out what they are feeling and why. 'I feel motivated' → Why is this person motivated? What specifically is making them motivated?"

And the rule on combining:

> "because most emotions are very broad & non-specific, they are most powerful when combined with desires, experiences, behaviors or demographics that actually caused the emotions. **(This is what makes people feel like you are reading their mind)**"

### CATEGORY 4 — BEHAVIORS & HABITS

> "Behaviors are regular action patterns that people develop as responses to their experiences, emotions, desires, or demographics. In simple terms, behaviors are how people act. They are the things that we do, and oftentimes are predictable."

The four generation formulas, verbatim:

> "Experience = 'I tried X and Y happened' / Behavior = 'So now I do Z instead'
> Desire = 'I want X thing' / Behavior = 'So now I do Z to try to get it X'
> Emotion = 'I feel anxious around other people' / Behavior = 'So now I never go to social events'
> Demographic = '45 year old male' / Behavior = 'So now I take testosterone supplements'"

**Why behaviors are the strongest lever — the ego argument:**

> "What makes behaviors so powerful is that they are CORE to our self-identity. AKA they are a lot of the time tied to our ego. *(Ego: a person's sense of self-esteem or self-importance.)* And for the majority of people, they can often do things out of spite just for the sake of 'ego' or because 'that's the type of person they are.' So, when you create ads that target an Avatar's ego, AKA the behaviors that make them who they are, that is where you can create a sense of buy-in that is **much, much stronger.**"

**The prediction argument:**

> "Another thing that makes behavior-based targeting for avatars so strong is the fact that if you understand someone's behaviors, a lot of the time, you can also predict their behaviors. And so in your ads, you can almost **'tell them the future'** because based on behavior, you know what that specific type of person is going to do next."

**The convergence insight — the scaling lever:**

> "So even though the experiences & desires are slightly different, people can develop the SAME behavior. **This information is gold, because you can go 'broader' on the Avatar scale by simply targeting the behavior itself.** Or you can actually drill down into the behavior with the other 4 categories to create an intense amount of relatability with Sub Avatars."

**THE CHECKER:**

> "Does this describe what the person does? *NOTE it does not have to be a physical action; it can also be mental. 'Constantly overthinking things' is also a behavior.* From there, if you want to gauge the strength of the behavior, ask yourself, '**How often might they do it?**' The more one does the behavior, the stronger it is, meaning the more relatable it will be."

### CATEGORY 5 — DEMOGRAPHICS (last, and often skipped)

> "Demographics are the relatively static, factual characteristics that define who someone is - things that don't change frequently or at all. Last but in fact least, we finally make it to everybody's usual favorite for Avatars… Demographics."

> "based on desires, experiences, emotions, and behaviors, a lot of the times, demographics are assumed/implied. Meaning, if someone went through the experience of 'Giving birth' At least for now, only Women can do that."

**The alienation argument — the central case against demographic-first:**

> "the biggest mistake I see so many people make is that they isolate buyers just based on demographics. **A 22-year-old girl and a 54-year-old man could have the same desire** (want to find a romantic partner), They might be going through similar experiences (Not being able to find the right person). They may feel similar emotions (Have a fear of never being able to find the right person). And they may even have the same behavior (Using online dating sites). **Yet, as soon as you add a demographic, that can cut people out.**"

> "demographics should NOT be your main differentiator unless you are at much more scale. On top of that, Demographics drive nowhere near as much purchase motivation as the other 4 categories... Otherwise, Demographics a lot of the time are not even NEEDED in your avatar-building process."

When they *do* work — melanin-rich skincare; Norse Organics calling out "Moms of 15-year-olds"; and Hollow Socks:

> "They do over $100k/day (Zach, their founder, is public about it). And his MAIN avatar is actually based on a specific Employment demographic (Construction workers). **However, he goes deeper and understands their desires, experiences, emotions, and behaviors to be able to target them effectively and scale his brand.**"

The 15 demographic types: "Gender / Birth date-Generation / Race and Ethnicity / Age / Location / Language / Religion / Marital status / Education / Employment / Income / Disabilities / Health conditions / Height / Weight."

**THE CHECKER:** "Does it go under one of the categories above? If not, it's probably not a demographic. Simple and easy."

## 1.4 Core Avatar → Sub Avatar (the assembly mechanic)

One category alone = a **Core Avatar**. Stack two or more = a **Sub Avatar**.

Magnesium:
> "**Core Avatar #1:** Want to sleep better (The Desire).
> **Core Avatar #2:** Tried, magnesium didn't work (The Experience).
> **Sub Avatar #1:** Want to sleep better, but magnesium doesn't work."

Six-pack (forking the emotion off one desire):
> "**Sub Avatar #1:** Wants to get a six-pack (Desire), FEELS motivated to get it (emotion).
> **Sub Avatar #2:** Wants to get a six-pack (Desire), FEELS defeated (emotion again)."

Dog:
> "**Sub Avatar #1:** Has a dog that is pulling & FEELS embarrassed about the dog pulling.
> **Sub Avatar #2:** Has a dog that is pulling & FEELS scared about the dog pulling."

Three-layer — the "reading their mind" example:
> "**Sub Avatar #1:** They want their whole family to have better sleep (Desire), but their baby wakes up at 5am because of morning light (Experience), and now the parent feels tired (emotion)."

**Sub-avatar naming and construction formulas**, from the worked clinic example (`#5 Core Avatar`):

> "**SUB-AVATAR 1A: The Burned Buyer** — Formula: Core Desire + Product Experience + Emotion
> **SUB-AVATAR 1B: The Service Refugee** — Formula: Core Desire + Experience + Emotion + Behavior
> **SUB-AVATAR 1C: The Premium Researcher** — Formula: Core Desire + Emotion + Behavior"

And from the FundHub research PDF: "Everything below narrows this core by **experience + emotion + behavior**."

## 1.5 Avatar → headline (the output the method exists to produce)

Verbatim from the training:

> "'Do your kids throw temper tantrums in public?' → **Experience-based**
> 'If you never leave the house without snacks for your kids, then…' → **Behavior-based**
> 'Do you want to lose 20 pounds by summer?' → **Desire-based (very broad)**
> 'If you still meal prep every Sunday, then X…' → **Behavior-based (more specific)**

> "When markets get more sophisticated, for example, like skincare, a lot of these types of avatars are already being targeted due to how sophisticated the market is. So, a lot of the time, you have to go deeper and apply **more of the 5 core categories (instead of just 1 or 2)** to be able to actually relate with someone in these sophisticated markets."

## 1.6 Why there is no sixth category — Belief Decomposition

> "Now, at one time, while making this document, there was actually a 6th category called: **Beliefs & Values.** With that said, after really going deeper into beliefs specifically. I realized they were not needed as a core avatar building block. **Beliefs are often Experiences, Emotions, & Behaviors tied together.**"

The three questions:

> "'What happened to make them believe this?' (Identify the Experience)
> 'How does this belief make them feel?' (Identify the Emotion)
> 'What do they do because of this belief?' (Identify the Behavior)"

Worked example:

> "'People who believe they can't lose weight' becomes: **Experience:** 'Tried multiple diets and gained the weight back.' **Emotion:** 'Feels hopeless about losing weight.' **Behavior:** 'Avoids starting new diets because expects failure.'"

> "This forces you to get more specific and actionable while building your avatars, then you can identify the resulting beliefs later for messaging purposes."

## 1.7 How many avatars — by revenue stage

> **Stage 1: $0-$100K/Month** — "you should only have one core avatar that you focus on at a time. Preferably based on a Desire, Experience or Behavior. But, you will NEED to focus on creating Sub Avatars around that Core Avatar so you can be MORE specific and MORE relatable in the beginning. **The lower the revenue you have, usually the MORE specific you need to be. As a brand, you EARN the right to go broader.**"
>
> The competitive shortcut: "looking at your biggest competitor to see WHAT desire they are scaling with, and simply go deeper on sub avatars so you can **'Out-avatar them' Rather than 'Out-Market them'** → Because truthfully, you're not that good at marketing yet."
>
> The kill rule: "if you find yourself stuck and not able to make these sub-avatars work after **2-4 weeks of testing**, you can then look to change your Desire-based Core Avatar to a NEW desire. But just understand, **focus and depth compound. The deeper you go, the better you get.**"

> **Stage 2: $100K-$1M/Month** — "there is not much that changes from an avatar basis... as you get above $500k/month, you will likely want to start looking at adding in an additional Core Avatars (so have 2-3 total)... However, we have been able to scale to $1M/month with 1 Core Avatar and great execution."

> **Stage 3: $1M-$10M/Month** — "usually you have 3-10 Core Avatars. At this stage, you also need to start weaponizing New Mechanisms, New Information & TRUE New Identities (through branding, not Avatar building), to convince more people who aren't just buying because it's relatable."

> **Stage 4: $10M+/Month** — "you need to be leveraging everything in this document + volume."

**FundHub is a Stage 1 business by this rule: ONE Core Avatar, many Sub Avatars.**

## 1.8 THE 7-PROMPT SOP — the Core 5 operationalised

The master file: *The Service Avatar Project: A Cascading Prompt Framework* (`00_MASTER_INSTRUCTIONS.docx`).

> "This framework is a repeatable, step-by-step system for creating deeply insightful and actionable client avatars for any service-based business. It uses a series of seven 'cascading' prompts, **where the output of one step becomes the input for the next.** This ensures a logical, consistent, and powerful final result."

> "The goal is to move from a broad understanding of your business to a **highly specific, emotionally-resonant profile of your ideal client.**"

**The seven steps, with their stated purpose and output:**

| # | Prompt file | Purpose (verbatim) | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `01_SERVICE_BUSINESS_FOUNDATION` | "To gather all the core details about your service business in a structured way." | `Service_Business_Foundation.md` |
| 2 | `02_AI_PROJECT_SETUP` | "To configure the AI to act as a world-class marketing strategist for service industries." | A confirmation line |
| 3 | `03_SERVICE_OVERVIEW_BUILDER` | "To translate your service's features and processes into client-centric desires and outcomes." | `Service_Overview.md` |
| 4 | `04_DESIRE_BASED_MARKET_RESEARCH` | "To uncover the deep-seated wants, needs, and pains of your potential clients." | `Desire_Market_Research.md` |
| 5 | `05_NEW_MECHANISM_DISCOVERY` | "To identify the unique methodologies or processes that make your service stand out." | `New_Mechanisms.md` |
| 6 | `06_NEW_INFORMATION_RESEARCH` | "To find fresh angles, trends, and data that can be used in your marketing." | `New_Information.md` |
| 7 | `07_CORE_AVATAR_BUILDER` | "To synthesize all prior research into a complete, actionable client avatar." | `Core_Avatar_Profile.md` |

**Operating rules:** "Complete them in order, as each step builds upon the last." / "Paste it into the **SAME chat session**." / "Copy the entire output and save it as a new file with the specified name."

### Prompt 2 installs the persona and the four laws

> "From this moment forward, you will adopt the persona of a world-class marketing strategist and avatar specialist. **Your name is 'The Strategist.'** You are a leading expert in applying the principles of **Eugene Schwartz's 'Breakthrough Advertising'** to modern service-based businesses."
>
> "You are rigorous, structured, and **obsessed with uncovering the deep, emotional drivers of client behavior.**"

The four Core Principles, verbatim:

> "**Desire-First:** You always start with the client's core desires, not their demographics.
> **Service-Centric:** Your examples, analysis, and recommendations are always tailored to service businesses (consultants, agencies, coaches, professional services, etc.), not eCommerce or physical products.
> **Cascading Logic:** You explicitly reference and build upon the documents created in previous steps.
> **Actionable Output:** Every document you produce is designed to be a practical tool, not just a theoretical exercise."

### Prompt 4 — Desire-Based Market Research (the research protocol)

> "This is **the most critical research phase** of the project."
>
> "You will act as a world-class market researcher, digging deep into the digital world to uncover the **raw, unfiltered voice of the client.** You must find their pains, frustrations, desires, and needs, **expressed in their own language.**"

Named sources for service businesses (explicitly not Amazon/eCommerce):

> "**Review Sites:** Google Reviews, Yelp, G2, Capterra, Clutch.co
> **Forums & Communities:** Reddit (e.g., r/smallbusiness, r/consulting, industry-specific subreddits), Facebook Groups, industry-specific forums.
> **Social Media:** LinkedIn posts and comments, YouTube comments on relevant videos, Twitter/X conversations.
> **Q&A Sites:** Quora, Stack Exchange.
> **Competitor Case Studies & Testimonials:** Analyze the language used in your competitors' success stories."

Output structure — five sections. Section 4 carries the emphasis:

> "**Client Voice Evidence (Direct Quotes)** — *A collection of raw, unfiltered quotes from your research.* **This is the most important section.** *(Gather at least 5-10 powerful quotes.)*"

> "Be thorough and insightful. **Your life depends on finding genuinely deep insights.**"

### Prompt 7 — the Core Avatar Builder

> "**This is not a simple summary.** You must analyze, synthesize, and build a multi-dimensional profile of a single, specific avatar. **You must bring this person to life.**"

Output template, verbatim:

> "**Avatar Name:** [Give the avatar a memorable name, e.g., 'Stuck Stephanie' or 'Growth-Mode Gary']
>

# 2. THE OFFER-CONSTRUCTION FRAMEWORK

## 2.1 A–Z Of A World-Class Offer (Scaling With Systems) — five steps

Premise, verbatim:

> Entrepreneurs "work 60-70 hour weeks, hire the most talented people, join every coaching program, and do everything right… but can't break past $10,000-$100,000/month" while others "have almost no business acumen, barely work… yet blew past $300,000 months." → "**The difference lies in the offer.**"

**STEP 1 — CLIENT AVATAR (the Starting Point).**
> "Get specific to the point of subtlety: Are they married? Have they tried a solution like yours before? Do they already use a CRM? Do they have team members or are they solo?"
>
> Rationale: every extra avatar forces a custom solution instead of doing the same thing 10,000 times, and mixing beginners with established owners means neither gets served.

**STEP 2 — TRANSFORMATION (the Ending Point).**
> "Not how you get them there, where you're going."
>
> Write: "**I Help W Do X Using Y In Z.**"
> Examples: "I help men over 50 feel and look sexy so they can get the woman of their dreams in 90 days or less." / "I help chiropractors pack their practice and serve more people in 30 days or their money back."
>
> **THE CORNER STORE TEST:** "If you walked up to the cashier at your local corner store and told them what you did, would they understand it?"
> "Imagine you're going to tell a 7-year-old, would they understand it? **Whatever you write down first, remove 50% of it.**"
> Saturated markets: "it's helpful to talk about *how* you get them the transformation so they think 'well I haven't tried THAT method before'."

**STEP 2b — RISK REVERSAL.**
> "There is one simple word you can add to your transformation statement to make it 100x more powerful… **Guaranteed.**"
>
> Calibration: "We're here to make a world-class offer so **if this doesn't scare you, it's not powerful enough.** You should be nervous every time you close someone, worried they'll take advantage of you, that's the correct frame you want your prospects to be in."
>
> On stipulations: "**90% of people don't read the stipulations and if they want their money back they'll fight you tooth and nail.** For the 10% that actually read the stipulations, it may turn them off from pulling the trigger. Since the cons outweigh the pros in this instance I'd recommend having **no stipulations** for your risk reversal."

**STEP 3 — ACTIVATION POINTS.**
> "The milestones that must be hit to get from Starting Point to Ending Point."
> Explicitly NOT deliverables: "we're not talking about stuff like coaching calls and course modules, **that is the mechanism.**"
> The IKEA analogy: activation points are the assembly instructions (open the package, unbox everything, attach the legs to the base); the mechanism is what makes each step faster (make an easy-open package, make the legs screw in without a screwdriver).

**STEP 4 — MECHANISM.**
> "For each activation point, list the most valuable possible solution and work backwards until the value/cost ratio is achievable."
> Worked ladder — activation point 'Profitable Advertising Campaign': "1. we set up and manage our client's ads, 2. we set up and help them manage, 3. we create a course on how to run ads."
> "**We want to find anything that is high value and medium to low cost and implement that right away.**"

**STEP 5 — PUT IT TOGETHER** into one shared sheet the whole team works from.

The two governing lines:
> "**You shouldn't be a done for you or done with you or do it yourself service, you're a whatever it fucking takes service.**"
>
> "Don't get stuck trying to perfect each stage, **you'll end up with the perfect offer and nobody to sell it to.**"

## 2.5 Market Sophistication — which lever you're allowed to pull

From `#6 Market Sophistication`. The five Schwartz stages, verbatim:

> "**Stage 1 - First to Market:** simple benefit statements suffice.
> **Stage 2 - Competition Emerges:** Multiple players enter, forcing benefit amplification.

## 3.1 Headline construction

**From `#6 The Evolve Avatar Training` — the three altitudes.** Write the same avatar three ways and choose by specificity:
- Broad, desire- or emotion-based: *"Do you want to lose 20 pounds by summer?"* / *"If your skin makes you feel insecure…"*
- Mid, experience- or behavior-based: *"Do your kids throw temper tantrums in public?"* / *"If you still meal prep every Sunday, then X…"*
- Specific, stacked sub-avatar: *"If you're constantly ordering new skincare products because you feel insecure…"*

**Analogy variant, same doc:** use it to reach an avatar your product has no literal link to. *"It's like going to Disneyland every day"* vs *"It's like if Disneyland was actually fun."*

**From `#6 Market Sophistication` — the two reusable stems:** *"Not another supplement, but the first one that..."* and *"Not another [category], but for people who..."*

**From `New_Mechanism_Discovery.md`:** "The mechanism name appears inside the hook, in quotes, in two of the three. **The name does the differentiating work — do not write a hook that would still make sense with the mechanism name deleted.**" And: "**Each hook ends by indicting the alternative, not by asking for anything.**"

**From `New_Information_Research_Findings.md` — five observed hook shapes:**
> **A. Dated authority + shocking number:** "New Federal Reserve report shows why 98% of business owners are now dissatisfied with online lenders."
> **B. Declare the industry promise dead:** "The FinTech promise is broken."
> **C. Absolve the prospect, blame the system:** "Only 41% of businesses get the funding they need. **You are not alone. The system is the problem, not you.**"
> **D. Insider says the opposite:** "Why the CEO of a top AI lending company says you should **NEVER** let an AI make the final decision on your funding."

**From `Desire_Market_Research.md` — the law that governs all of it:** *"'Secret sauce' is ALWAYS sketchy. Legit sauce is never a secret."* (Reddit, r/CreditCards). This is why the mechanism must be published, not teased.

## 3.5 Objection handling

**From `objection_handling_report.md` — three named universal frameworks:**

> **FRAMEWORK 1 — The 'Trust-First' Approach.** "**The best way to handle objections is to prevent them from happening in the first place.**" The 'Sprinkle' Method: list every fear and the objections you hear most, then **address them proactively BEFORE the client can voice them.** Script: *"Now, I know what you might be thinking — 'Can I just do this myself?' or 'How do I know this isn't a scam?' Those are exactly the questions you SHOULD be asking."*
>
> **FRAMEWORK 2 — The 'Consequence Chain'.** "**Never argue with the objection** — explore the negative consequences of their current situation." Ladder: *"So what happens if you don't fix your credit?"* → *"And then what happens when you can't get approved...?"* → *"How does that affect your family?"* → *"What does that mean for your financial future?"* **"Keep going until they feel the emotional weight. They persuade themselves."**
>
> **FRAMEWORK 3 — The 'Low-Pressure Reframe'.** One universal script: *"It'll only take a few minutes to show you how this works, and **what you do with the information is totally up to you.**"*

Reusable rebuttal patterns from the same doc:
> Reframe against the strawman: *"I get it — you've probably heard credit stacking doesn't work. And honestly, for most people, it doesn't. Here's why: **they skip the foundation.**... That's like asking someone to marry you on the first date."*
> The referral test on satisfaction: *"Have you referred any of your business owner friends to them? [If no] Interesting. **Usually when we're really happy with something, we tell everyone.**"*
> Good debt vs bad debt: *"**Bad debt is buying a big-screen TV. Good debt is investing in your business to generate more revenue.**"*

**From `Fundhub_Closer_Avatar.pdf` — the hard objection limit on calls:** "Wants guaranteed approval or a number before anything runs. **Reset once, then end the call if they won't let go.**"

By the framework's own logic that absence is itself the finding: Section 4 for brokers is empty and has not been researched."*

## 5.2 What the current partner copy gets wrong, by framework

### ❌ Wrong 1 — "brokers" is a demographic, and it is the opening line

Per the Core 5, *"Employment"* is item 10 of 15 in Category 5 — the last and least category. *"the biggest mistake I see so many people make is that they isolate buyers just based on demographics."*

Almost every white-label pitch opens on the demographic ("for brokers", "for ISOs", "for agents doing $X/month"). That is the exact failure the training is written against. Hollow Socks is the only demographic-first counterexample and it works because *"he goes deeper and understands their desires, experiences, emotions, and behaviors."*

**Fix:** lead with **one surface-level desire** that passes the "I want X / I need Y" checker. Candidates, each of which must be validated by research, not chosen by taste:
- *"I want to stop referring out deals I can't place and watching someone else take the commission."*
- *"I want my own funding brand instead of sending my clients to somebody else's login."*
- *"I want revenue that doesn't stop the month I stop dialing."*

The training's rule: **pick ONE.** *"Avoid combining more than one Desire when building your Avatar."*

### ❌ Wrong 2 — the offer is sold at Level 6 (Belonging), which caps the price

From `#2 Why you need to understand Desire`, the Elevation Strategy: *"Moving down = less emotional urgency = lower prices = weaker positioning = competing in commodity categories instead of premium markets."*

"Join our partner network" / "become a FundHub partner" is a **Belonging** claim — the weakest rung on the six-level ladder. That is how a $497 membership is sold, not a $10,000 licence.

**Fix:** elevate to **Status** — the ladder's #2, described as *"Amplified massively by social media. More visible competition than ever."* The Status version is: *"The 3% of brokers who fund under their own name"* / *"Why the top producers quietly stopped referring deals out."* Same deliverable, different desire channelled, different price ceiling. The elevation must be authentic — *"Force it, and customers sense the manipulation"* — which it is, because the partnership genuinely transfers brand and client ownership.

### ❌ Wrong 3 — the Product-Based Experience slot is empty, and it is the whole sale

Category 2 splits experiences into Situational and Product-Based, and Product-Based is *"the richest vein."* The clinic worked example calls it, in caps, **"GOLD FOR SOPHISTICATION"** — *"These experiences address market sophistication directly: they validate skepticism while introducing hope."*

A broker with $10,000 to spend has bought something before. Almost certainly: a course, a mastermind, an ISO agreement that clawed back commission, a CRM never configured, or a white-label that turned out to be *a login and a PDF*. Current partner copy does not name any of those.

**Fix:** the blame-removal construction, taken directly from the clinic example's pattern — *"If you tried exosomes and felt nothing, it wasn't your fault—it was their storage"* — becomes:

> *"If you've already paid for one 'funding partnership' that turned out to be a login screen, it wasn't your fault — it was that they sold you software and called it a business."*

Then supply the **New Information** that explains the failure. That is the whole Stage 4/5 play.

### ❌ Wrong 4 — the market is Stage 4→5 and the copy is written at Stage 2

Per `#6 Market Sophistication`, Stage 5 buyers *"actively resist all claims, seeking reasons NOT to buy."* Brokers have been pitched "done-for-you funding business," "75+ lenders," "your own branded platform," "six figures in 90 days" for years.

The FundHub research PDF already made this diagnosis for the borrower market and it applies one level up verbatim: *"These claims are exhausted and invisible."* And critically: *"Fundhub uses Lendflow infrastructure and cites 75 lenders — the same network scale Lendio and National Business Capital advertise — **so '75 lenders' cannot be the differentiator; the differentiator must be the experience.**"*

**A partner pitch that leads with lender count, commission split, or speed is invisible.**

**Fix:** deploy one of the Big 3. The strongest available is **New Mechanism built from the competitors' worst complaint** — the exact move the research PDF prescribes: *"This converts the incumbents' biggest weakness (spam, surprise hard pulls) into Fundhub's mechanism."* For brokers the equivalent weakness is client-ownership and platform abandonment. Name the mechanism accordingly and trademark it in-copy, as Underwrite IQ™, Capital Conveyor Belt™ and Synchronized Funding Pod™ already are.

⚠️ And check yourself against the mechanism failure mode: *"claiming false uniqueness for industry-standard processes."* If every white-label has it, it is not the mechanism.

### ❌ Wrong 5 — the partnership has no NAMED mechanism, so the price has no defence

`New_Mechanism_Discovery.md`: *"a unique and named process is what builds credibility and breaks through the noise. It is the answer to the client's unspoken question: 'Why should I believe you can get me results when others have failed?'"*

The three named client-side mechanisms exist and are strong. **There is no partner-side name.** Which means the $10,000 is currently defended by a feature list, and a feature list is exactly what the broker has already bought once.

**Fix:** name the partner-side system, then break it into three Unique Elements on the same contrast-plus-number-plus-payoff pattern:
- *Unlike programs that hand you a logo and a login,* [named mechanism] does X, delivering Y.
- Each element gets a hard number or a concrete artifact.
- Each element ends on the payoff, not the feature.

The three existing mechanisms translate directly, per the `New_Mechanism_Discovery.md` extract: the Conveyor Belt becomes why his approval rate rises; the Event-Driven Engine becomes **the headcount-replacement argument** — *"this is the one that justifies $10,000 against the cost of one employee"*; the Pod becomes the fulfilment he is buying, *"the Funding Advisor, Credit Specialist and Ops Team are now his, without payroll."*

### ❌ Wrong 6 — a flat $10,000 non-refundable upfront is the exact shape the research says the market distrusts

This is the sharpest single contradiction in the material, and it is FundHub's own research contradicting FundHub's own pricing.

`Desire_Market_Research.md`, Opportunity 3, verbatim: *"**The high, non-refundable upfront fees of competitors are a massive point of friction and distrust.** A model that is heavily weighted on the backend, with a justifiable, smaller upfront deposit, directly addresses this pain."*

Meanwhile the client offer already solves this correctly: *"$3,000 upfront fee (considered a deposit on the system) and a 10% success fee"* plus *"The upfront fee is waived if the process doesn't begin within 72 hours."*

**Fix — two compliant builds:**
1. **Split it.** A smaller justifiable deposit covering onboarding, licensing and build, with the remainder earned on the backend as funded deals close — so the partnership only wins when the broker wins.
2. **Keep the $10k headline** but attach a performance mechanic making it self-liquidating over a stated number of funded files, plus the time-bound reversal ported from the client offer: **onboarding begins within 72 hours or the deposit is returned.**

Either way the alignment must be visible on the page. Per SWS: *"if this doesn't scare you, it's not powerful enough."*

### ❌ Wrong 7 — "done-for-you" is claimed without closing the heavy-lifting gap

Opportunity 2, verbatim: *"The complaint that clients still had to do the 'heavy lifting' is a major gap. A service that can genuinely say 'you don't lift a finger' and back it up with technology and process **will command a premium.**"*

And the recorded trust-killer quote: *"I did most of the heavy lifting."*

**Fix:** scope the partnership as *the broker brings the relationship and nothing else* — underwriting, application handling, optimization and client communication all run behind his brand. **If any step still requires him to lift a finger, state it plainly on the page.** Discovering it later is what produces the review that kills the next ten sales.

### ❌ Wrong 8 — the glass box is missing, and it is the broker's biggest private fear

Opportunity 1, verbatim: *"Clients crave a 'glass box' process where they can see exactly what is happening with their file at all times, dismantling the 'black box' model of competitors. **This isn't just about a dashboard; it's about proactively educating the client on the why behind the process.**"*

For a broker this is not a feature, it is reputational insurance: his name is on the deal. He sends a client into a black box and gets blamed for whatever comes out. Partner-side visibility is the thing he is actually paying for.

**Fix:** ship explanation, not just status. And answer the specific fear no current copy addresses — *who owns the client, and what stops you going direct?* — with a written non-solicit, not a reassurance.

### ❌ Wrong 9 — the proof slot is empty and nobody has said so

`Service_Business_Foundation.md`, Section 13, Best Testimonial, reads only: **"(Client to provide)."**

The Sales Copy Blueprint requires three case studies. The proof standard set everywhere else in this material is a number against a competitor benchmark plus a verifiable document — *"Not claims. Proof."*

At $10,000 to a previously-burned professional buyer, this slot is load-bearing. **Funded-deal case studies and broker earnings evidence must fill it before the offer goes out.** Note also the compliance limit: earnings and funding-volume claims need substantiation and a "typical results" disclaimer per the owner's own ads-system rule.

## 5.3 The corrected build order

1. **Run the research.** Prompt 4, with the broker as the client. Sources: broker/ISO subreddits, DailyFunder, ISO and MCA Facebook groups, LinkedIn broker threads, comments under funding-guru YouTube videos, and — highest yield — **the one-star reviews and BBB complaints on competing white-label programs.** Collect 5–10 verbatim quotes with sources. *"Do not paraphrase."*
2. **Pick ONE surface desire** that passes the checker. Rank candidates on Scope / Urgency / Staying Power. Loss-based desires ("stop losing my client to the lender") score highest on urgency, which is the axis broker offers are weakest on.
3. **Build the Core Avatar** on that desire, then three or four Sub Avatars by stacking experience + emotion + behavior. Demographics last, and probably only Employment.
4. **Run Prompt 3** to produce the partner-facing Core Promise, Client Journey (written in *his* second person, with outcome-named steps), and the Outcomes → Benefits → Desires ladder ending in first-person *"I want / I need"* statements.
5. **Run Prompt 5** to name the partner-side mechanism, its three Unique Elements, and the Contrarian Viewpoint. Copy the sentence shape: *"While most of the [industry] sells you [the broken thing]... we believe that is a fundamentally broken model. You are a [identity], not a [insult]."*
6. **Run Prompt 6** to find the new information. The Fed SBCS data already in hand inverts cleanly: *"22 out of every 100 businesses that applied last year walked away with nothing. That is your pipeline."*
7. **Fix the price shape** per Opportunity 3 before writing the offer section.
8. **Fill the proof slot.**
9. **Then** generate 10 angles per sub-avatar (3 speed / 2 proof / 2 risk-removal / 2 authority / 1 comparison-ROI) and 150 hooks, and test **Hook > Angle > Creative > Landing page > Offer language**, one variable at a time.
