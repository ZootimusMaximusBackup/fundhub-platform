# Branch recovery — 2026-08-01

Seventeen remote branches were deleted from `ZootimusMaximusBackup/fundhub-platform`. All seventeen were recovered.

**Nothing was lost.** Every deleted branch tip was found, restored as `recover/<original-name>`, and pushed to origin. No merges, no rebases, no deletions were performed.

## How they were found

All 17 came from **Source 1 — the GitHub Activity API**
(`repos/ZootimusMaximusBackup/fundhub-platform/activity?activity_type=branch_deletion`).
Each deletion record carries a `before` field, which is the exact commit the branch pointed at when it was deleted.

Sources 2 (local reflog / unreachable objects) and 3 (pull request refs) were not needed. Every recovered commit was still present in the local object store here, so the branches were rebuilt directly with no network fetch of lost objects.

## How to read the two "ahead" numbers

These branches were cut from an older `main` and never caught up. So a plain file-by-file comparison against today's `main` shows hundreds of differences that are just `main` having moved on — not work sitting on the branch. Two numbers matter:

- **Commits ahead of main** — `git rev-list --count main..<sha>`. Real commits not in `main`.
- **New files not in main** — files the branch has that today's `main` does not have at all. This is the work genuinely at risk.

A branch can be ahead by real commits and still show zero new files. That means the *content* of its work already reached `main` by another route (a different branch, a re-do, a merge), even though this exact commit never did. Those are marked below.

Every one of the 17 tips is technically unmerged — none is an ancestor of `main`.
